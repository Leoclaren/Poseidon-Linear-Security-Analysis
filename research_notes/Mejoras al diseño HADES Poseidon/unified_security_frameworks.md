## Key Question 1: What is SoK 2026/1931 and what does it cover?

### Takeaway
"SoK: Zero-Knowledge Friendly Hash Functions over Prime Fields" (Bouvier, Grassi, Koschatko, Rechberger, Schmid, Steiner, Wu, Yan; IACR eprint 2026/1931, received 8 Sept 2026, approved 12 Sept 2026) is confirmed to be a genuine systematization-of-knowledge covering (a) algorithmic design paradigms and modes of operation for ZK-friendly/arithmetization-oriented (AO) hash functions, (b) a consolidation of algebraic cryptanalysis techniques against them, (c) a review of third-party cryptanalysis per design, (d) explicit cost analysis across three arithmetization styles — R1CS, AIR, PLONK — both theoretically (constraint/gate counts) and empirically (benchmarks), and (e) a released open-source toolkit (SageMath/Python reference, Rust, in-circuit implementations). I could not access the paper's body text to confirm the exact taxonomy of attack classes, the comparison-table contents mapping each design to which attacks it resists, or the verbatim recommendations/open-problems text.

### Cited Findings
- Full author list: Clémence Bouvier (Université de Lorraine, CNRS, Inria, LORIA), Lorenzo Grassi (Eindhoven University of Technology), Katharina Koschatko (TU Graz), Christian Rechberger (TU Graz), Fabian Schmid (TU Graz), Matthias Johann Steiner (independent researcher), Zhuo Wu (Ant Group, Digital Technologies), Hailun Yan (University of Chinese Academy of Sciences) — [eprint 2026/1931 abstract page](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1931)
- Dates: received 8 September 2026, approved 12 September 2026; license CC BY — [eprint 2026/1931 abstract page](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1931)
- Abstract-level framing: the paper addresses that "traditional hash functions are often inefficient within ZK protocols due to their high constraint complexity in arithmetic circuits," that "various ZK-friendly hash functions have been proposed" but "their diverse algebraic structures and varying security margins make it difficult for practitioners to select the optimal primitive," and that the paper performs a systematic survey covering "both algorithmic design paradigms and modes of operation," consolidates algebraic cryptanalysis methods, and evaluates constructions across R1CS, AIR, and PLONK — [eprint abstract via web-fetch](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1931); corroborated independently by [IACR News item, 12 September 2026](https://iacr.org/news/item/29544)
- IACR News item additionally states: "The study consolidates algebraic cryptanalysis methods and reviews third-party security analysis across various designs. For constructions vulnerable to known attacks, researchers determined minimum round requirements to achieve comparable security levels." It also states the paper provides "theoretical analysis — deriving constraint and gate counts across different arithmetization approaches (R1CS, AIR, PLONK) — and empirical benchmarks," with an open-source suite: "a SageMath/Python reference framework, native Rust implementations, in-circuit code, and reusable documentation resources" — [IACR News item, 12 September 2026](https://iacr.org/news/item/29544)
- A general web search independently reproduced the same abstract-paraphrase: "The paper conducts a systematic survey of ZK-friendly hash functions, covering both algorithmic design paradigms and modes of operation, consolidates the relevant algebraic cryptanalysis techniques, and reviews the third-party cryptanalysis of each design" — [search result summary](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1931)
- A secondary search-engine paraphrase (not independently verifiable against the PDF body) states the survey "covers three arithmetization paradigms: R1CS, classical PLONK, and AIR," and that "the term 'Arithmetization-Oriented' (AO) is used as an umbrella term for primitives tailored to efficient realization in proof systems, distinguishing between R1CS-friendly, PLONKish-friendly and other variants" — [search snippet, unverified against primary text](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1931.pdf)

### Inferences
- Given the author roster (Bouvier, Grassi, Rechberger, Steiner are all core Poseidon/Poseidon2/HADES-lineage authors; Koschatko, Schmid are TU Graz cryptanalysts active on recent Poseidon attack papers), this SoK is very likely written partly in direct response to, and citing, the 2026 wave of full-round/adaptive-matrix attacks (Vitto 2026/1579; Merz & Rodríguez García 2026/306; and related papers found below), making it the most authoritative and current unified reference available for a HADES-style design's security argument — but this is an inference from authorship and timing, not a confirmed textual citation.
- The statement that the paper "determined minimum round requirements to achieve comparable security levels" for "constructions vulnerable to known attacks" strongly suggests the SoK contains a round-count/attack-resistance comparison table across named designs (Poseidon, Poseidon2, Rescue, Griffin, Anemoi, etc.), consistent with the task's expectation, but I cannot quote its actual contents.

## Key Question 2: Are there other 2025-2026 SoK/survey/unified-criterion papers besides 2026/1931?

### Takeaway
I found no other paper explicitly titled "SoK" or "systematization of knowledge" for this exact design space (HADES-style/AO permutations) in 2025-2026 besides 2026/1931. The closest adjacent/overlapping work is new-design papers (Arion, RainHash2.0, XHash, pSquare-hash) that each perform their own partial multi-design comparison (typically vs. Poseidon/Poseidon2/Anemoi/Griffin/Rescue) as part of their "related work," not a standalone systematization. There is also a companion/competing SoK in the adjacent sub-area of backdoor risk ("Nothing Up My Matrix," 2026/1901) that functions as a focused SoK-adjacent security note rather than a general unified framework.

### Cited Findings
- "Arion: Arithmetization-Oriented Hashing for Zero-Knowledge Proof Systems" (eprint 2026/1842) is described in search results as specifying secure parameters for prime fields and evaluating "across several arithmetization frameworks (R1CS, Plonk, and AIR) compared with prominent AO hash functions including Poseidon, Poseidon2, Anemoi, Griffin, and Rescue" — [eprint 2026/1842 via search snippet](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1842)
- "Boosting Efficiency and Security in Arithmetization-Oriented Hashing for Zero-Knowledge Proof Systems" (eprint 2026/1271) is described as analyzing how "arithmetization-oriented compression functions minimize multiplicative complexity over the framework's native field, making them significantly more efficient than bit-oriented designs in algebraic circuits" — [eprint 2026/1271 via search snippet](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1271)
- "The XHash family for ZK-friendly hash functions" appears as a Springer Design, Codes and Cryptography journal article (2026) — [link.springer.com DOI 10.1007/s10623-026-01875-1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10623-026-01875-1) — found only as a title in search results; content not fetched (out of primary scope given budget, and secondary to 2026/1931 per task priority).
- "Nothing Up My Matrix: Kleptographic Backdoors in ZK-friendly Hash Functions" (eprint 2026/1901, authors Hyunsik Jeong, Malte Sander Leip, Mincheol Son; received 6 Sept 2026, approved 10 Sept 2026) shows that "adversarial use of this freedom can create a kleptographic attack surface" in matrix selection, and that "a malicious designer controlling matrix selection can choose a matrix that maps a chosen input to a chosen output" while the "resulting matrix is MDS and passes the additional matrix security checks required by the target primitive" — [eprint 2026/1901 via search snippet + IACR news item 10 Sept 2026](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1901)
- No ToSC/EUROCRYPT/CRYPTO/USENIX/CCS 2025-2026 paper with "SoK" or "systematization" in the title specific to AO hash functions turned up in searches beyond 2026/1931 and the backdoor-adjacent 2026/1901.

### Inferences
- The absence of a competing general SoK suggests 2026/1931 is currently the field's sole comprehensive unified reference, making it unusually important as a citation anchor — consistent with the task's framing that it is "the single most important source."
- 2026/1901 (kleptographic backdoors) is a complementary but narrower "unified" contribution: it is effectively a systematized warning about an entire class of designs ("any MDS matrix is admissible") rather than a full taxonomy of cryptanalytic attack classes. A HADES-style design's security argument should probably cite both 2026/1931 (general framework) and 2026/1901 (matrix-selection transparency/backdoor risk) if matrix generation procedure is discussed.

### Gaps

## Key Question 3: Does the SoK (or anything found) discuss the tension between GRS21-style internal-matrix-only criteria and the newer full-round/adaptive-matrix attack class (Vitto 2026/1579; Merz & Rodríguez García 2026/306)?

### Takeaway
I could not confirm from the SoK's actual text whether/how it frames this tension (body text inaccessible — see Access Limitation above). However, I independently confirmed the two anchor attack papers exist exactly as described in the brief, and found at least three further 2026 papers in the same lineage (round-skipping/S-box-skipping, and an adaptively-chosen-MDS full-round collision) that make the "tension" concrete: all of them show that security arguments restricted to the partial-round linear layer (the GRS21/branch-number style criterion, which is also the exact subject of this repository's own CLAUDE.md proof) are insufficient once full rounds and/or adversarial matrix choice are brought into scope.

### Cited Findings
- Giuseppe Vitto, "Slipway: Accessing Finite Subspace Trails in Poseidon," eprint 2026/1579 (LayerZero Labs / University of Luxembourg; received 2 Aug 2026, revised 18 Sept 2026). The attack "exploit[s] finite subspace trails in the Poseidon algebraic permutation," using "constrained input families paired with custom MDS matrices" to "guide these families through initial full rounds into designated trails where S-box operations add minimal algebraic degree" — described as showing full rounds can act as "controlled reparametrization rather than as a source of degree growth." Targets Poseidon's KoalaBear instance (t=16, α=3, R_F=8, R_P=20); constructs a family achieving degree 3^10 instead of the expected 3^28 over 18 rounds, yielding a full CICO-2 solution "using MDS matrices satisfying Poseidon's official checks" — [eprint 2026/1579](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1579)
- Simon-Philipp Merz (ETH Zurich) & Àlex Rodríguez García (UPC), "Algebraic Attacks on Poseidon2(b)," eprint 2026/306 (Feb 2026). This is a "round-skipping algebraic attack exploiting structural weaknesses in linear layer matrices and operational modes," targeting Poseidon2/Poseidon2b. Key results: scenarios where "finding preimages is easier than solving the corresponding CICO problem" (a first for Poseidon2(b)); "the first algebraic collision attack that outperforms its algebraic preimage counterpart"; for specific 128-bit parameters, improvement "by a factor of 2^106" over prior algebraic collision attacks; despite this, "the primitive falls short of its claimed security level," and mitigation strategies are discussed "without efficiency penalties" — [eprint 2026/306](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/306)
- Further corroborating/extending attacks in the same 2026 lineage found via search (not deep-read, titles/snippets only):
  - "Midpoint Reset: A Full-Round Poseidon Collision from an Adaptively Chosen MDS Matrix," eprint 2026/1760 — gives "an explicit compression collision ... for all 28 rounds of the KoalaBear Poseidon instance with parameters (t,α,R_F,R_P)=(16,3,8,20), in a setting where round constants are fixed before the MDS linear layer is chosen," by "constructing a single linear layer that simultaneously controls two executions through both the full and partial rounds by tracking their midpoint and half-difference" — [eprint 2026/1760](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1760)
  - "From Round Skipping to S-Box Skipping: Attacking Poseidon's Partial Layer via Subspace Restriction," eprint 2026/1692 — presents "GSR, a generalized S-box skipping gadget that absorbs a single initial full round and t-2k partial rounds without increasing the polynomial degree of the Poseidon polynomial system" — [eprint 2026/1692](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1692)
  - "Cryptanalysis of Poseidon-Based Fiat-Shamir Protocols," eprint 2026/409 — title/topic found but not read — [eprint 2026/409](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/409)
- On stated mitigations/repair paths for the Midpoint Reset / adaptive-matrix class, a search-engine synthesis (not a primary-source quote, treat as secondary paraphrase) reports: "switch back to MDS matrices (as Khovratovich showed the four-round skips cannot exist under an MDS linear layer), circulant MDS layers built from Reed-Solomon codes with manageable performance loss, add full rounds at roughly 15% overhead, or a hybrid of Poseidon1 full rounds with Poseidon2 partial rounds" — [search synthesis, secondary/unverified](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1760.pdf)
- The Ethereum Foundation's "Poseidon Cryptanalysis Initiative" (poseidon-initiative.info) is an active, funded (Phase 2 running 2024-2026, concluding December 2026) research program explicitly testing "whether Poseidon/Poseidon2 are secure for high-value applications and foundations of Ethereum," with bounty programs for CICO/Density/Zero-test problems and a Collision Prize running through January 2029 ($992,000 total, escalating $32K-$512K by partial-collision size), plus an "Attack Reward Program" (up to $90,000 for peer-reviewed improved-attack papers) and "Groebner Basis Exploratory" grants — [poseidon-initiative.info](https://www.poseidon-initiative.info/). I explicitly checked this page for any reference to the SoK (2026/1931) or to the Vitto/Merz-García/Midpoint-Reset/round-skipping papers and found **none** — the public initiative page content I could access does not mention them by name.

### Inferences
- Taken together, the Vitto (1579), Merz & Rodríguez García (306), Midpoint Reset (1760), and GSR round/S-box-skipping (1692) papers constitute a coherent and growing 2026 attack class that specifically defeats security arguments based solely on the branch-number/MDS criterion applied in isolation to the partial-round linear layer (i.e., exactly the GRS21-style scope) — because they all use full rounds, adaptively-chosen MDS matrices, or cross-round structural coupling as the attack surface, which a purely-local MDS criterion on the partial round sub-matrix does not account for. This is directly relevant to the CLAUDE.md proof in this repository: that proof's invariant-subspace argument is itself a *local*, within-partial-rounds argument (confined to the subspace V={(0,0,δ)}) — it does **not** by itself rule out the Vitto/Midpoint-Reset style of attack, which operates by choosing the *full-round* behavior and/or the matrix adaptively across the full+partial round boundary, not merely by violating MDS within the partial-round matrix alone. In other words: proving MDS is necessary for partial-round-local security (as this repo's CLAUDE.md does) does **not** establish that MDS is sufficient for full-permutation security once adaptive/adversarial matrix choice or full-round coupling is in scope — that is a strictly separate (harder) claim that the 2026 attack papers show can fail even under nominally "MDS, checks-passing" matrices.
- Since I could not read 2026/1931's body, I cannot report whether it explicitly makes this "necessary vs. sufficient" distinction as a named methodological point, but given it is dated after 2026/1579 and 2026/306 (both earlier in 2026) and co-authored by matrix/security specialists (Grassi, Steiner) actively working on this exact question, it is highly likely to engage with it substantively. This should be flagged as a priority re-check if full-text access is obtained later.

### Gaps
- No confirmed primary-source statement (from 2026/1931 itself) on how it recommends resolving the GRS21-vs-full-round tension — this is the single biggest gap relative to the task's explicit ask, caused entirely by the Cloudflare/Wayback access limitation documented above, not by absence of relevant material in the literature.
- Did not verify whether the Ethereum Poseidon Cryptanalysis Initiative has issued any post-hoc public statement (blog, mailing list, GitHub issue) responding specifically to Vitto/Merz-García/Midpoint-Reset; the initiative's own landing page as fetched does not reference them, but a dedicated announcements/blog subpage might (not checked, to stay within budget).

## Key Question 4: Does the SoK (or any other 2025-2026 paper) explicitly discuss the R1CS vs. Plonkish vs. native cost-model distinction (i.e., caution that one backend's optimization may not transfer to another)?

### Takeaway
Yes — multiple independent search-engine syntheses of the SoK's content consistently describe it as explicitly treating R1CS, AIR, and PLONK as distinct cost models with distinct constraint/gate-counting rules, which is exactly the methodological caution the task is looking for; I could not pull a verbatim quote, but the claim is corroborated by three independent sources describing the same paper, plus general background describing why the three backends diverge.

### Cited Findings
- IACR News item (independent of the search-engine paraphrases) states the SoK provides "theoretical analysis — deriving constraint and gate counts across different arithmetization approaches (R1CS, AIR, PLONK) — and empirical benchmarks" — [IACR News item, 12 September 2026](https://iacr.org/news/item/29544)
- A search-engine synthesis of the PDF (secondary, unverified against primary text, but specific and plausible) states: "The simplest cost model is the Rank One Constraint System (R1CS), where cost is dictated primarily by the number of multiplications. The term 'Arithmetization-Oriented' (AO) is used as an umbrella term for primitives tailored to efficient realization in proof systems, distinguishing between R1CS-friendly, PLONKish-friendly and other variants," and separately that "for backends, the Poseidon2 realization has substantially lower arithmetization cost and prover cost compared to other hash implementations" — [search synthesis of 2026/1931](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1931.pdf)
- A separate search synthesis on the general R1CS/Plonkish distinction (not from 2026/1931, general background corroborating why the caution matters) states: "PLONKish's custom gates often replace hundreds or thousands of R1CS constraints with O(1) degree-2/3 constraints per operation. PLONKish achieves elegance through custom gates that capture complex operations in single constraints, while AIR's homogeneous structure fundamentally mismatches certain instruction sets" — [search synthesis, general background](https://veridise.com/blog/learn-blockchain/zk-fundamentals-intermediate-representations/) (Veridise blog, "ZK Arithmetization: R1CS, Plonkish and CCS Explained" — not read directly, title/snippet only)
- Griffin and Anemoi's own papers (prior work, not 2026) are noted in a search synthesis as comparing "performance when used in various configurations and proof systems, showing [a] huge advantage when used in R1CS-based zero-knowledge proof systems" specifically — i.e., the backend-dependence of claimed efficiency gains is already a known theme in the immediate prior literature that 2026/1931 is systematizing — [search synthesis](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1931.pdf)

### Inferences
- This corroborates the user's methodological point directly: multiple independent sources agree the SoK treats R1CS/AIR/PLONK as separate cost models requiring separate constraint/gate derivations rather than a single unified cost metric, which is the strongest available secondary evidence (short of the primary text) that 2026/1931 explicitly supports "an optimization for one backend may not help another" as a stated methodological caution for the field.

### Gaps
- No verbatim quote of the SoK explicitly warning designers in these words; the claim rests on three convergent secondary paraphrases rather than primary text, due to the access limitation above.

### Cited Findings

- The absence of any found "resists both X and Y by construction" design claim suggests either (a) this is a genuinely open gap in the 2025-2026 literature — i.e., no one has yet published a from-scratch HADES-style permutation whose design paper explicitly targets both attack classes as named adversary models — or (b) such a claim exists in a paper not surfaced by my searches (e.g., buried in a late-2026 eprint not yet well-indexed, or in the SoK's own "open problems" section, which I could not read).
