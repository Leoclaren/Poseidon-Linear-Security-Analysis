# Internal/Partial-Round Matrix (M_I) Design Criteria for HADES-Style Permutations (Poseidon/Poseidon2/Griffin/Neptune), 2024-2026 Beyond GRS21

Baseline reference for all comparisons: Grassi, Rechberger, Schofnegger, "Proving Resistance Against Infinitely Long Subspace Trails: How to Choose the Linear Layer," IACR Transactions on Symmetric Cryptology 2021(2):314-352, DOI 10.46586/tosc.v2021.i2.314-352 — cited in this note as GRS21. Eprint listing found via search: https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/500 (title and ToSC venue match exactly; I was not able to independently re-confirm this eprint number against the paper's own PDF header during this session, so treat the eprint number, not the ToSC citation, as provisional — see Gaps below). The GRS21 sufficient (not necessary) check used throughout industry code is: **the characteristic polynomial of M_I^i must be irreducible of degree t for every i = 1..2t** (sometimes extended to i = 1..4t in some tooling).

## Is there a 2024-2026 paper giving a NECESSARY AND SUFFICIENT condition for M_I to avoid all invariant-subspace-style weaknesses, including finite (not just infinite) subspace trails?

### Takeaway
Yes, in a qualified sense: Enyan Li and Gaoli Wang, "Revisiting Linear Subspace Trails in Poseidon" (eprint 2026/967), split the problem into two cleanly separated regimes — infinite trails (connected to invariant subspaces of the linear layer, as in GRS21) and finite trails (bounded by a new rank-growth argument) — and give an exact bound on the maximum length of a finite linear subspace trail conditional on the linear layer not admitting an invariant subspace. This is the closest 2024-2026 result to a necessary-and-sufficient characterization, but it is explicitly conditional (it assumes the "no infinitely-long trail / no repeated diagonal entries causing invariant subspace" case first), so it is not a single unconditional necessary-and-sufficient criterion replacing GRS21 — it is a two-part criterion (GRS21-style infinite-trail exclusion, plus a new finite-trail length bound) that together give a fuller picture than GRS21 alone.

### Cited Findings
- Li & Wang (eprint 2026/967), received 2026-05-15, last revised 2026-06-24, authors affiliated with the Software Engineering Institute, East China Normal University — paper "revisits infinite and finite linear subspace trails in Poseidon-like designs" and "provides quantitative heuristic estimates for the probability of such invariant subspace conditions under random parameter choices." — [eprint 2026/967](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/967)
- Their main finite-trail bound: in a general setting with state width t and s S-box coordinates activated per round, if repeated diagonal entries in the lower-right block do not give rise to infinitely long linear subspace trails, then the maximum length of a finite linear subspace trail is exactly ⌈t/s⌉ − 1; for Poseidon (s = 1) this is exactly t − 1 consecutive linearized partial rounds. — [eprint 2026/967](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/967)
- The paper also revisits the connection between infinitely long linear subspace trails and invariant subspaces of the linear layer specifically for the Cauchy MDS matrices used in Poseidon. — [eprint 2026/967](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/967)
- Giuseppe Vitto (LayerZero Labs / University of Luxembourg), "Slipway: Accessing Finite Subspace Trails in Poseidon," eprint 2026/1579, last revised 2026-09-18, states finite subspace trails "exist for every linear layer" — i.e., finite trails cannot be eliminated by any choice of M_I; the paper's contribution is showing how to make them *reachable/exploitable* by jointly engineering a constrained input family and a custom MDS matrix so the full rounds act as "controlled reparametrization" rather than a source of degree growth ("full-round absorption"), applied concretely to KoalaBear (t=16, α=3, R_F=8, R_P=20) to get degree 3^10 instead of the expected 3^28 and a full CICO-2 solution. — [eprint 2026/1579](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1579.pdf)
- Related 2025 work: Grassi, Koschatko, Rechberger, "Poseidon and Neptune: Gröbner Basis Cryptanalysis Exploiting Subspace Trails," ToSC 2025(2):34-86, eprint 2025/954, shows that Gröbner-basis attacks exploiting subspace trails (to linearize some partial rounds) outperform plain Gröbner-basis attacks against Poseidon/Poseidon2 and Neptune — i.e., subspace-trail-based linearization remains a practically superior attack vector even against designs believed GRS21-compliant, reinforcing that "no infinite trail" (GRS21) does not imply "no exploitable finite trail." — [eprint 2025/954](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/954.pdf)

### Inferences
- The 2024-2026 literature's answer to "is GRS21 sufficient" is effectively "no": GRS21 only rules out *infinite* trails (invariant subspaces of M_I). Finite trails of length up to t−1 (Li & Wang) always exist regardless of matrix choice, and Vitto's 2026/1579 result shows these finite trails can be made cryptographically relevant (full CICO breaks) by an attacker who controls or predicts the input family and (in the attack's construction) the MDS matrix jointly — meaning matrix choice for the *external* (full-round) MDS layer also matters to internal-round security, not just M_I in isolation.
- No source found in this session presents a single, unconditional, checkable necessary-and-sufficient test for "M_I is immune to all invariant-subspace-style weaknesses" — the state of the art is a layered set of checks (GRS21 infinite-trail exclusion + Li/Wang finite-trail length bound + case-by-case reachability analysis à la Vitto) rather than one closed-form criterion.

## What criteria does Poseidon2 (eprint 2023/323) use for choosing μ_0...μ_{t-1} in M_I = J + diag(μ_i − 1), and has any paper found this insufficient or proposed a stronger alternative?

### Takeaway
Poseidon2's stated criterion is (1) M_I invertible and (2) M_I^i has an irreducible characteristic polynomial of degree t for i = 1..2t (ruling out the GRS21-style subspace trails) — and this criterion has been empirically shown **insufficient or fragile in practice** by at least two independent 2024-2026 incidents: (a) Consensys gnark-crypto's "mamabear" parameter set shipped diagonals that failed the condition when the same diagonal values were reduced modulo a different prime, and (b) Plonky3's KoalaBear width-32 parameters fail the documented irreducibility condition for M_I^k at every k=1..64, relying instead on a weaker, as-yet-undocumented property ("e_0 is a cyclic vector of M_I") whose sufficiency is an open question raised by the community, not resolved by the Poseidon2 paper itself.

### Cited Findings
- Poseidon2 (eprint 2023/323) defines the internal matrix as M_I = J + diag(μ_i − 1), where J is the all-ones matrix; the internal-matrix condition requires M_I invertible and M_I^i to have irreducible characteristic polynomial for i = 1..2t, which is stated to rule out the subspace trails described in Section 5.2 of that paper; this condition is explicitly a property of the **pair** (diagonal, prime), not of the diagonal values alone. — [eprint 2023/323](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/323) (page ~15 per search-engine-extracted summary; I could not independently re-confirm page number from the primary PDF, which returned 403/404 on direct fetch in this session)
- Direct empirical insufficiency/fragility evidence #1 (gnark-crypto, Consensys-Incorporated): PR #929 "fix(mamabear): replace invalid Poseidon2 diagonals, narrow digest to 6" — the mamabear parameter set's diagonals failed the internal-matrix condition because they had been ported from KoalaBear's diagonal values but reduced modulo the mamabear prime, producing a matrix with a **reducible characteristic polynomial at i=1** for both width-16 and width-24 configurations; the PR states that in this failing case "the internal matrix fixes a proper subspace and the subspace trails [from the Poseidon2 paper] are not ruled out." The fix adds a new generic checker `HasNoInvariantSubspace` (Faddeev–LeVerrier characteristic-polynomial computation + Rabin irreducibility test, modeled on the reference implementation's `check_minpoly_condition`), wired into a `TestInternalMatrixCondition` test for every field so future diagonals cannot be ported across primes without re-validation. — [gnark-crypto PR #929](https://github.com/Consensys-Incorporated/gnark-crypto/pull/929)
- Direct empirical insufficiency/fragility evidence #2 (Plonky3): GitHub issue #2588 "KoalaBear Poseidon2 width 32: how was the internal diagonal chosen?" documents that the width-32 diagonal **fails** the documented sufficiency condition in `p3-poseidon2/src/internal.rs` ("the characteristic polynomial of M_I^k is irreducible for k=1..2t") — specifically, the characteristic polynomial of M_I is reducible for every k=1..64, with observed factorization degrees 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 9, 11 (versus the width-16 case, where the condition holds). The issue author notes that, despite this, "e_0 is a cyclic vector of M_I," meaning no M_I-invariant subspace lies in the hyperplane {x_0 = 0} — a weaker property that might still block the specific GRS21 attack even though the primary irreducibility criterion fails. The issue explicitly asks (unanswered in the thread as fetched): how the width-32 diagonal was actually selected/verified, whether the failure is "known and accepted... because the finer Grassi-Rechberger-Schofnegger trail algorithms pass," and whether documentation/parameters should be updated. — [Plonky3 issue #2588](https://github.com/Plonky3/Plonky3/issues/2588)
- Poseidon2 fixes M_I by canonical special-case choices for t=2 and t=3 (distinct from the general t≥4 construction) for performance reasons, per the reference implementation description. — summarized from search-engine extraction of [HorizenLabs/poseidon2](https://github.com/HorizenLabs/poseidon2) and related search results; direct repository fetch was not performed in this session.

### Inferences
- The two incidents above (gnark-crypto mamabear, Plonky3 KoalaBear-32) show that the GRS21/Poseidon2 irreducibility criterion is routinely violated in shipped, real-world implementations when parameters are reused or adapted across primes/widths without re-deriving the diagonal from scratch for the new (prime, width) pair — i.e., the criterion is correct in theory but operationally fragile, because it is non-transferable across primes and was in practice not always re-verified before shipping.
- The Plonky3 case suggests the community is informally exploring a *weaker* alternative sufficient condition ("e_0 is a cyclic vector of M_I under the restricted map on {x_0=0}") when the full irreducibility condition fails, but as of the fetched GitHub thread this is an open, unresolved question raised by a community member — not a peer-reviewed or formally established stronger/alternative criterion from a 2024-2026 paper.

### Gaps
- I did not find a published 2024-2026 *paper* (as opposed to a GitHub issue) that formally proposes and proves a stronger/alternative sufficient condition replacing the Poseidon2 irreducibility check; this may exist (e.g., it's plausible Li & Wang 2026/967 or Vitto 2026/1579 address it indirectly) but I could not confirm this from the abstract-level content retrieved.
- I was unable to directly fetch the full PDF text of eprint 2023/323 (both the abstract-page URL and the ia.cr/2023/323.pdf short-link failed to return parseable text — 403/404), so the "page ~15" and exact Section 5.2 wording are sourced from a search-engine-level extraction rather than a primary-source quote I verified myself. This should be treated as moderately, not fully, reliable.

## Are there known-good, publicly documented M_I matrix FAMILIES with concrete parameters for t=3 to t=24?

### Takeaway
The clearest documented family is Poseidon2's own M_I = J + diag(μ_i − 1) construction with HorizenLabs' reference implementation fixing canonical special-case diagonals for t=2 and t=3 and a general construction for t≥4 (concrete values live in the reference repo, not independently re-verified in this session); beyond that, I did not find a distinct 2024-2026 paper publishing a new, independently-proposed M_I matrix *family* (as opposed to a verification criterion) with concrete parameter tables spanning t=3 to t=24.

### Cited Findings
- HorizenLabs/poseidon2 (https://github.com/HorizenLabs/poseidon2) is cited across multiple 2024-2026 discussions (gnark-crypto, Plonky3) as the canonical reference implementation providing concrete M_I diagonals per field/width; Poseidon2 fixes special-case M_I values for t=2 and t=3 distinct from the general t≥4 construction. — summarized from search results referencing [HorizenLabs/poseidon2](https://github.com/HorizenLabs/poseidon2); not independently fetched/verified in this session.
- Plonky3 (https://github.com/Plonky3/Plonky3) and gnark-crypto (https://github.com/Consensys-Incorporated/gnark-crypto) both ship concrete per-field, per-width M_I diagonals (e.g., KoalaBear t=16 and t=32, Mersenne/BabyBear-family "mamabear" t=16/t=24) derived from or adapted from the Poseidon2 construction — but as shown above, at least two of these concrete instances (KoalaBear t=32, and the ported mamabear diagonals) failed the documented verification criterion, so "publicly documented" is not synonymous with "verified-good" in all shipped cases. — [Plonky3 issue #2588](https://github.com/Plonky3/Plonky3/issues/2588); [gnark-crypto PR #929](https://github.com/Consensys-Incorporated/gnark-crypto/pull/929)

## Is there a known connection between M_I's "maximal partial-round rank growth" property (dim K_r(M) = t−r exactly) and security, beyond what Vitto 2026/1579 already established?

### Takeaway
A closely related rank-growth notion appears independently in at least two other 2024-2026-adjacent places — Li & Wang's eprint 2026/967 "rank growth condition" (bounding finite trail length at t−1 when rank growth is maximal and no invariant subspace exists) and khovratovich/poseidon-tools' `_algorithm_2`/`_algorithm_3` (operationalizing "does the subspace generated by repeated M-application on e_0 reach full dimension t") — but I could not retrieve Vitto 2026/1579's own exact dim K_r(M) = t−r statement or theorem text in this session (PDF fetch was blocked), so I cannot confirm precisely how these three treatments relate to each other beyond their clear topical overlap.

### Cited Findings
- Vitto, eprint 2026/1579 ("Slipway: Accessing Finite Subspace Trails in Poseidon"): abstract-level content retrieved states finite subspace trails "exist for every linear layer," and frames the paper's contribution as showing how such trails can be made attack-relevant via joint engineering of input family and MDS matrix (full-round absorption) — but the specific "dim K_r(M) = t−r" formalism named in the task prompt was not visible in the content successfully fetched (direct PDF fetch returned HTTP 403; the abstract page fetch succeeded only at summary level). — [eprint 2026/1579](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1579.pdf)
- Li & Wang, eprint 2026/967: the "rank growth condition" under which the maximum finite-trail length is exactly t−1 (for Poseidon, s=1) is the clearest candidate match for the dim K_r(M) = t−r property referenced in the task — maximal rank growth (reaching full rank/dimension t as fast as possible, i.e., losing the trail after t−1 rounds) is presented as the condition that makes the t−1 bound tight. — [eprint 2026/967](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/967)
- khovratovich/poseidon-tools `_algorithm_2`: directly computes "the subspace generated by repeatedly applying M to the basis vector e_0, checking whether the dimension reaches t before stabilizing" — an executable, real-world test of exactly this rank-growth property, used as bounty-program verification tooling. — [khovratovich/poseidon-tools, mds_matrix.py](https://github.com/khovratovich/poseidon-tools/blob/main/poseidon/mds_matrix.py)

### Inferences
- All three sources (Vitto 2026/1579, Li & Wang 2026/967, and the poseidon-tools verifier) converge on the same underlying idea: the speed at which repeatedly applying the partial-round matrix to a seed vector (e_0 or the active S-box coordinate) spans the full state space is the key quantity governing both (a) how many rounds can be linearized "for free" by an attacker and (b) whether that linearization is exploitable. This is consistent with — and independently corroborates — the dim K_r(M) = t−r framing in the task prompt, even though I could not directly quote Vitto's own statement of it.
