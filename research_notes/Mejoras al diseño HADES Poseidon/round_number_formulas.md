# Round-Number and Security-Margin Formulas in HADES-Style AO Permutations (Poseidon family)

## What is the original Poseidon (Grassi et al., USENIX Security 2021 / eprint 2019/458) formula for R_F and R_P, and what attack classes does it budget a security margin against?

### Takeaway
The original Poseidon paper derives R_F and R_P from four explicit, independently-computed bounds — statistical (differential/linear), interpolation, Gröbner-basis, and "other" (truncated/impossible differential, rebound) attacks — takes the maximum requirement across all of them, and then adds an *arbitrary* fixed security margin of **+2 full rounds and +7.5% partial rounds**. For the flagship x⁵-instances the final formula collapses to R_F = 8 (fixed) and R = R_F + R_P with R_P determined by whichever attack class binds, expressed concretely as R = 56 + ⌈log₅(t)⌉ (M=128), R = 35 + ⌈log₅(t)⌉ (M=80), R = 111 + ⌈log₅(t)⌉ (M=256), all for ≈256-bit fields (BLS12-381 / BN254 / Ed25519 scalar fields). [Source: eprint.iacr.org/2019/458 (PDF, read directly)](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/458)

### Cited Findings
- Round structure: R_F = 2·R_f full rounds (R_f at the start, R_f at the end, each with t S-boxes) and R_P partial rounds in the middle (1 S-box + (t−1) identities), total R = R_F + R_P. — [Poseidon, eprint 2019/458, §2.2](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/458)
- Statistical (differential/linear) bound, Eq. (2): at least 6 rounds with full S-box layers are necessary; formally
  R_F < 6 if M ≤ (⌊log₂p⌋ − C)·(t+1), else R_F < 10, where C = log₂(α−1) for S(x)=xᵅ (C=2 for α=5, C=1 for α=3), and C=2 for S(x)=1/x. — [Poseidon, eprint 2019/458, §5.5.1, Eq. (2)](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/458)
- Interpolation attack bound, Eq. (3)–(4): for S(x)=xᵅ, R ≤ ⌈log_α(2)·min{M, log₂p}⌉ + ⌈log_α t⌉; for S(x)=1/x, ⌊R_F·log₂(t)⌋ + R_P ≤ ⌈log₂ p⌉ + ⌈0.5·min{M, log₂p}⌉ + ⌈log₂ t⌉. — [Poseidon, eprint 2019/458, §5.5.2, Eqs. (3)-(4)](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/458)
- Gröbner-basis attack bound, Eq. (5)–(6): for S(x)=xᵅ, R ≤ log_α(2)·min{M/3, log₂(p)/2} and R ≤ t − 1 + min{log_α(2)·M/(t+1), log_α(2)·log₂(p)/2}; an analogous (more complex) pair of inequalities (Eq. 6) holds for S(x)=1/x involving both ⌊R_F·log₂(t)⌋ and R_P separately. — [Poseidon, eprint 2019/458, §5.5.2, Eqs. (5)-(6)](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/458)
- "Other attacks" (truncated differentials, impossible differentials, rebound attacks) are claimed to be covered by the same round count required against statistical (differential/linear) attacks — no separate formula is derived for them. — [Poseidon, eprint 2019/458, §5.5.1 "Other Attacks"](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/458)
- Final security margin (§5.4): "Given the minimum number of rounds necessary to provide security against all attacks known in the literature, we arbitrarily decided to add (1) two more rounds with full S-box layers, and (2) 7.5% more rounds with partial S-box layers," i.e. **+2 R_F and +7.5% R_P**. — [Poseidon, eprint 2019/458, §5.4](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/458)
- Worked formulas after applying the margin, x⁵-Poseidon over ≈256-bit p (Propositions 5.1–5.3): Poseidon-128: R_F=6 (statistical/Gröbner derivation), R=R_F+R_P=56+⌈log₅(t)⌉; Poseidon-80: R=35+⌈log₅(t)⌉; Poseidon-256: R=111+⌈log₅(t)⌉. — [Poseidon, eprint 2019/458, §5.3, Propositions 5.1–5.3](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/458)
- Concrete instantiations (Table 2, over BLS12-381/BN254/Ed25519 scalar fields, S-box x⁵): Poseidonπ-128 uses (t=3, R_F=8, R_P=57) and (t=5, R_F=8, R_P=60); Poseidonπ-80 uses (t=3, R_F=8, R_P=33) and (t=5, R_F=8, R_P=35); Poseidonπ-256 uses (t=6, R_F=8, R_P=120) and (t=10, R_F=8, R_P=120). — [Poseidon, eprint 2019/458, Table 2](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/458)
- Invariant subspace trails are handled structurally, not by adding rounds: the linear-layer MDS matrix M is generated (via Cauchy-matrix search, Grain LFSR, self-shrinking mode) and explicitly vetted via the algorithm of "Weak Linear Layers in Word-Oriented Partial SPN and HADES-Like Schemes" (eprint 2020/500) to rule out any invariant/iterative subspace trail (with or without active S-boxes) persisting for all R_P partial rounds — this is a feasibility *constraint on matrix choice*, separate from the round-count formulas above. — [Poseidon, eprint 2019/458, §2.3 "Avoiding Insecure Matrices"](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/458)
- Zero-sum partitions are explicitly *not* budgeted for: "we do not make claims about the security of Poseidonπ against zero-sum partitions," justified because the gap between rounds coverable by a zero-sum partition and rounds breakable via preimage/collision is judged not relevant in practice. — [Poseidon, eprint 2019/458, §5.5.2 "(We Do Not Care About) Zero-Sum Partitions"](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/458)

### Inferences
- The formula is explicitly a "take the max of four independent attack-derived lower bounds, then add a flat margin" design, not a single closed-form expression — this is the structural pattern that Poseidon2, Poseidon2b, and essentially every successor in this family (Rescue-Prime, Griffin, GMiMC) replicate with their own attack-specific sub-formulas.
- R_F is effectively fixed at 8 (statistical minimum 6 + margin 2) across essentially all parameter sets in the original paper regardless of t or M, while R_P is the parameter that scales with t, M, and field size log₂(p) — this is the pattern later papers (Poseidon2, bounty attacks) continue to interrogate.

### Gaps
- The exact closed-form general equation combining Eqs. (2)-(6) into one "master formula" for arbitrary (t, α, M, p) is not reproduced in full in the paper body (it is deferred to eprint 2019/458 Appendix, which was not independently fetched in this research pass) — only the per-case worked instances (Propositions 5.1–5.3, Table 2) were directly verified.

## Did Poseidon2 (eprint 2023/323) or Poseidon2b (eprint 2025/1893 / IACR CiC 2(4) 2026) change this formula, and why?

### Takeaway
Poseidon2 keeps the same attack-driven structure (statistical ⟹ R_F margin, interpolation + Gröbner-basis ⟹ R_P via a max-then-margin rule) but recalibrates the partial-round formula with an explicit **R_P ≥ ⌈1.075 · max(R_interp, R_GB)⌉** and a refined interpolation bound **R_interp = ⌈min{κ,n}/log₂(d)⌉ + ⌈log_d(t)⌉ − 5** that scales with field bit-size n = ⌈log₂ p⌉; it also fixes R_F = 8 (statistical minimum 6, +2 margin — unchanged from Poseidon1). Poseidon2b (binary-extension-field variant, 2025/1893, published as IACR Communications in Cryptology 2(4), 2026) keeps the Poseidon2 SPN/round structure but re-derives the *interpolation and Gröbner-basis complexity estimates from scratch* using the **ideal degree** as the standard measure, because binary extension fields introduce attack vectors (Frobenius/field-structure-exploiting subspace trails) that do not arise in the prime-field setting, and because recent cryptanalytic advances changed how algebraic complexity should be estimated.

### Cited Findings
- Poseidon2 partial-round bound (as implemented/cited by a gnark-crypto maintainer PR reproducing the paper's Eq. 1): R_P ≥ ⌈1.075 · max(R_interp, R_GB)⌉, with R_interp = ⌈min{κ, n}/log₂(d)⌉ + ⌈log_d(t)⌉ − 5, where n=⌈log₂p⌉ (field bit-size), κ = target security level, d = S-box degree, t = state width. — [gnark-crypto PR #900, "fix(mamabear): derive the Poseidon2 round numbers for this field"](https://github.com/Consensys-Incorporated/gnark-crypto/pull/900)
- R_F is set to 8 throughout Poseidon2 ("the paper's value throughout"), described as "+2 margin over the statistical minimum of 6" — i.e., unchanged from the original Poseidon formula/margin. — [gnark-crypto PR #900](https://github.com/Consensys-Incorporated/gnark-crypto/pull/900)
- Concrete small-field Poseidon2 round counts derived from this formula: for the "KoalaBear" field (n≈31 bits) R_P=20 (saturates at n=31); for "MamaBear" (n≈49 bits) R_P=32, with the interpolation bound R_interp=29 being the binding constraint — illustrating "a wider field needs more rounds." — [gnark-crypto PR #900](https://github.com/Consensys-Incorporated/gnark-crypto/pull/900)
- Poseidon2b abstract: "Due to the close similarity to their prime field counterparts, many existing cryptanalytic results directly carry over to Poseidonb and Poseidon2b. Nevertheless, we revisit the security analysis to incorporate recent advances in cryptanalysis and to account for attack vectors that do not arise in the prime field setting. In particular, we focus on algebraic cryptanalysis and subspace trails, techniques that resulted in attacks on initial versions of Poseidon defined over binary extension fields. Our complexity estimates are based on the ideal degree, now increasingly adopted as a standard measure in algebraic cryptanalysis." — [Poseidon(2)b, IACR CiC 2(4), 2026 / eprint 2025/1893, Abstract](https://cic.iacr.org/p/2/4/15/pdf)
- Poseidon2b Contribution #2 (verbatim): "We revisit the security analysis of Poseidon(2)b from scratch. In particular, we confirm the density estimates of the polynomial representation, which help establish lower bounds on the complexity of the interpolation attack and reanalyze the security against algebraic attacks. We base the attack complexity on the ideal degree bound, which is now becoming a standard in Gröbner basis cryptanalysis, additionally incorporating different security notions. We have confirmed the analysis through an extensive set of practical experiments that cover various attack scenarios." — [Poseidon(2)b, §1.1 "Our Contributions"](https://cic.iacr.org/p/2/4/15/pdf)
- Historical note from Poseidon2b on binary-field predecessor Starkad: Starkad (the original Poseidon paper's withdrawn binary-field variant) was withdrawn by its own designers partly due to "the incapacity of providing a good estimation of the growth of the degree, a problem later on solved in [CGG+22]" — i.e., binary-field degree-growth estimation was historically broken and had to be separately fixed before Poseidon2b could be built with confidence. — [Poseidon(2)b, §1.2 "Related Works"](https://cic.iacr.org/p/2/4/15/pdf)
- Poseidon2b targets a 128-bit security level specifically, designed for use with binary extension field proving systems such as Binius. — [Poseidon(2)b, Abstract](https://cic.iacr.org/p/2/4/15/pdf)

### Inferences
- Poseidon2's main formula change relative to Poseidon1 is not a change in attack-class coverage (same four classes) but a *tightening/recalibration* of the R_P formula's constants (the −5 term, the 1.075 multiplier applied to the max rather than per-class margins of +7.5%) and an explicit field-bit-size-aware interpolation bound — this matters most for the newer small-field (KoalaBear/BabyBear/Goldilocks-class, ~31–64 bit) instances Poseidon2 specifically targets, which were not covered by Poseidon1's ≈256-bit-field-only worked examples.
- Poseidon2b's departure from Poseidon1/Poseidon2 is primarily about the *measurement methodology* (ideal degree bound instead of the older complexity estimates) rather than the round-count formula's shape — it is a methodological update driven by the broader field's 2024-2025 shift toward ideal-degree-based Gröbner cryptanalysis (see next question), applied specifically to the binary-extension-field setting where Poseidon1's original prime-field-only analysis does not directly apply.

### Gaps
- The exact Gröbner-basis bound R_GB formula used in Poseidon2 (analogous to Poseidon1's Eq. 5-6) was not directly extracted from the primary Poseidon2 (eprint 2023/323) PDF — eprint.iacr.org blocks automated PDF fetches (Cloudflare challenge + 403 on the WebFetch tool for this specific file), so this number comes from a secondary source (a GitHub PR implementing the paper's formulas), not the paper text itself. The Poseidon2b exact post-ideal-degree R_P formula (its final concrete numeric equation) was likewise not directly extracted — only the qualitative contributions are confirmed from the paper's abstract/intro, since later pages of the 2025/1893 PDF (where the formula would appear, likely §4-§5) were not fetched in this pass due to tool-call budget constraints.

## Have any 2025/2026 papers shown the established round-number formulas need MORE rounds than previously thought, because of a newly discovered attack class?

### Takeaway
Multiple 2025-2026 eprint papers introduce new attack techniques (finite/restricted subspace trails reachable through full rounds, round-skipping via weak-matrix structure, degree-annihilation, improved bivariate resultants) that **reduce the effective algebraic degree or break more rounds than previously modeled for specific reduced-round or small-field instances** — but in every case found, the authors explicitly state these results stay *within* the existing security margin (do not break full, un-reduced Poseidon/Poseidon2/Poseidon2b at their claimed security levels) and none of the sources retrieved state a revised/larger round-number formula is now required for production parameter sets. Several (CheapLunch, bivariate resultant, bounty-claiming papers) are explicitly framed as progress toward the Ethereum Foundation's Poseidon bounty program, which targets small-field (M31/KoalaBear, Goldilocks-class) and reduced-round instances specifically, not full BN254/BLS12-381 production instances.

### Cited Findings
- **Slipway: Accessing Finite Subspace Trails in Poseidon**, Giuseppe Vitto, eprint 2026/1579 (received Aug 2, 2026; approved Aug 3, 2026): constructs finite subspace trails that pass through the *full-round prefix* of Poseidon (not just partial rounds) using MDS matrices that pass the Poseidon designers' own matrix-security checks, yet still allow a finite trail to reach the partial-round region with degree far below generic expectation; this yields a complete CICO-2 solution. — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/1579](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1579)
- Slipway concrete result: for the **KoalaBear** Poseidon instance with (t=16, α=3, R_F=8, R_P=20), the polynomials representing the first two output coordinates reach algebraic degree 3¹⁰ instead of the generically-expected 3²⁸ — i.e. the first four full rounds and the next fourteen partial S-boxes contribute *no* degree growth for this specific constrained family. — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/1579 (PDF content, via WebFetch summary)](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1579)
- **Skipping Class: Algebraic Attacks exploiting weak matrices and operation modes of Poseidon2(b)**, Simon-Philipp Merz and Àlex Rodríguez García, eprint 2026/306: "We present new algebraic attacks on Poseidon2 and Poseidon2b. We exploit the specific structure of the matrices that define the linear layers... which allows us to improve round-skipping for the CICO problem... we show how to boost our round-skipping attack when directly modelling algebraic preimage attacks of Poseidon2(b) in compression and sponge mode... we describe the first algebraic collision attack that outperforms its algebraic preimage counterpart. We improve over state-of-the-art algebraic attacks for a range of parameters, e.g. for one recommended 128-bit parameter set we improve over previous state-of-the-art algebraic collision attacks by a factor of 2¹⁰⁶. **However, due to the algebraic security margin this does not mean the primitive falls short of its claimed security level.** Finally, we discuss how our attacks can be mitigated without affecting the efficiency of Poseidon2(b)." (verbatim abstract) — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/306](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/306)
- **Poseidon and Neptune: Gröbner Basis Cryptanalysis Exploiting Subspace Trails**, Lorenzo Grassi, Katharina Koschatko, Christian Rechberger, eprint 2025/954 (published IACR ToSC 2025(2)): shows that Gröbner-basis attacks exploiting subspace trails (which linearize some partial rounds) can outperform basic GB attacks against Poseidon/Poseidon2 and Neptune; identifies that Neptune's inverse external rounds keep the same degree as forward rounds (unlike Poseidon's, whose inverse round functions have high degree), making Neptune comparatively more exposed to this specific exploitation. — [eprint.iacr.org/2025/954](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/954)
- **The Algebraic CheapLunch: Extending FreeLunch Attacks on Arithmetization-Oriented Primitives Beyond CICO-1**, Bak, Bariant, Boeuf, Briaud, Øygarden, Phanse, eprint 2025/2040: extends "FreeLunch"-style attacks beyond the CICO-1 problem; applied to Poseidon, Neptune, and XHash8 with experimentally verified assumptions; delivers new polynomial modelings, more efficient initial Gröbner-basis computation, and improved complexity estimates for the change-of-ordering (FGLM) step "derived from tighter upper bounds on the ideal degree." — [eprint.iacr.org/2025/2040](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2040)
- **Claiming bounties on small scale Poseidon and Poseidon2 instances using resultant-based algebraic attacks**, Bak, Bariant, Boeuf, Hostettler, Jazeron, eprint 2026/150: in response to the Ethereum Foundation's November 2024 Poseidon/Poseidon2 bounty program, the authors "found solutions to the first 3 proposed instances of Poseidon2-31m and Poseidon2-31k" plus "the first two Poseidon-256 instances," using a new resultant-based approach for Poseidon2 and univariate root-finding for Poseidon-256 — these are explicitly the *small/reduced* bounty instances, not full production parameter sets. — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/150](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/150)
- **A Better Bivariate Resultant Attack on Round-Reduced Poseidon**, Antoine Bak and Maël Hostettler, eprint 2026/1905: adapts a bivariate-resultant algorithm to solve Poseidon CICO-2 systems faster than generic by exploiting their special structure, achieving (under heuristics) time quasi-linear in D_I^(1−1/w) (2≤w<2.38, matrix-multiplication exponent) rather than the degree bound predicted generically; explicitly targets *round-reduced* instances and, per available summaries, did not break any full-round version. — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/1905](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1905)
- **From Round Skipping to S-Box Skipping: Attacking Poseidon's Partial Layer via Subspace Restriction**, Amit Singh Bhati, Sundas Tariq, Tomer Ashur, eprint 2026/1692 (Aug 15, 2026): presents "GSR," a generalized S-box-skipping gadget that absorbs one initial full round and t−2k partial rounds without increasing the Poseidon polynomial system's degree (state size t, input/output constraints 2k); yields a probability-1 distinguisher over t−2k+1 rounds, and is parameterized purely by (t,k), applying "regardless of the choice of round constants, MDS matrix, S-box exponent α, or field size p." Concrete impact: CICO-1 solved over **28 of 31 rounds** and CICO-2 over **25 of 31 rounds** for the specific Poseidon instance previously proposed for LeanVM. — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/1692](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1692)
- **Top Gun: Degree Annihilation Attacks on Poseidon**, Antonio Sanso and Giuseppe Vitto, eprint 2026/1254: introduces "degree annihilation" — rather than removing rounds from the algebraic model (round-skipping), it imposes algebraic constraints that force dominant degree terms to vanish, yielding polynomial systems of substantially lower effective degree; applied as proof-of-concept to reduced-round Poseidon instances to obtain new CICO-2 attacks. — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/1254](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1254)
- "Resultants Meet Resultant: Improving CICO-1 and CICO-2 Attacks" — another 2026 eprint paper surfaced in search results but not independently verified in this pass; flagged for completeness as likely relevant but unconfirmed in detail. — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/1281](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1281)
- "Midpoint Reset: A Full-Round Poseidon Collision from an..." — a 2026 eprint paper title surfaced repeatedly in search results suggesting a full-round Poseidon *collision* result; title alone suggests this could be the most significant finding in scope (a full-round break), but the paper's content was **not** independently fetched/verified in this research pass due to tool-budget constraints — flagged as a priority gap. — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/1760](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1760)
- Poseidon Initiative bounty-tracking site confirms the bounty program's purpose is explicitly "to ensure that the interpolation attack is the fastest preimage attack on Poseidon" and "to verify that the complexity of the interpolation attack on the reduced-round versions matches the theoretical estimates" — i.e. the bounty program is a validation exercise for the existing formula, not evidence the formula is broken. Claimed reduced-round breaks listed include Poseidon-256 (RF=6,RP=8) at ~24-bit security (claimed 9 Dec 2024), Poseidon-256 (RF=6,RP=9) at ~28-bit (claimed 31 Dec 2024), Poseidon-64 (RF=6,RP=7) and (RF=6,RP=8) (claimed Apr-May 2025), Poseidon-64 (RF=6,RP=10) (claimed 24 May 2025), and Poseidon-31/KoalaBear-class M31 variants (RF=4,RP=0). — [poseidon-initiative.info](https://www.poseidon-initiative.info/)

### Inferences
- The 2025-2026 literature cluster (Slipway, Skipping Class, From Round Skipping to S-Box Skipping, Top Gun, CheapLunch, the resultant/bounty papers) represents a coordinated wave of attacks that all push the "effective degree is lower than the generic/original formula assumed" line of argument — they are converging on the idea that the *original interpolation/Gröbner bounds implicitly assumed worst-case (random-like) degree growth per round*, and that structured subspace-trail/round-skipping/degree-annihilation techniques show real degree growth is sometimes much slower for specific constrained sub-problems (CICO-k), which is a genuinely new attack *class* relative to Grassi et al. 2021's four original categories.
- None of the retrieved abstracts claim a concrete revised R_F/R_P formula replacing the original/Poseidon2 ones for production (128-bit, BN254/BLS12-381, un-reduced) instances — the quantitative "breaks" are consistently on (a) explicitly round-reduced instances, (b) small/toy fields (KoalaBear ~31-bit, M31, Goldilocks-class), or (c) framed as improvements to attack *algorithms* that still land inside the existing security margin at full round counts. This is a meaningfully different situation from, e.g., a hypothetical paper proving the Grassi et al. margin itself insufficient at full rounds for a shipped 128-bit BN254 instance — no such claim was found in this pass.
- The one paper title that could represent an exception — "Midpoint Reset: A Full-Round Poseidon Collision" (eprint 2026/1760) — was not verified and should be treated as the single highest-priority item for a follow-up research pass, since a genuine full-round collision (if the title is accurate and not about a toy/reduced parameter set) would be the first result directly falsifying the current round-number formulas' sufficiency.

### Gaps
- eprint 2026/1760 ("Midpoint Reset: A Full-Round Poseidon Collision...") was not fetched/read in this pass — its actual scope (full-round meaning full R_F+R_P at a real security level, vs. a reduced/toy instance mislabeled "full-round" within its own reduced context) is unknown and should be verified before treating it as evidence the formulas are insufficient.
- "Resultants Meet Resultant" (eprint 2026/1281) content was not verified beyond the title appearing in search results.
- None of the eprint.iacr.org PDFs for 2026/306, 2026/1579's full text, 2025/954, 2025/2040, 2026/150, 2026/1905, 2026/1692, or 2026/1254 could be directly downloaded in this pass (eprint.iacr.org returns HTTP 403 to the WebFetch tool and serves a Cloudflare bot-challenge page to direct curl requests); all quantitative claims for these papers above come from third-party search-result summaries/abstracts rather than a direct read of the full PDF text, except where a WebFetch summary of PDF content is explicitly cited (Slipway, Poseidon2b, original Poseidon, which were fetched successfully via different routes — usenix.org and iacr.asia mirror / direct PDF download succeeded where eprint.iacr.org direct PDF fetch failed). This is a meaningful reliability caveat: exact round numbers "broken" for most 2026 papers beyond Slipway/S-Box-Skipping (28/31, 25/31 rounds) and Skipping Class (2^106 factor) were not independently confirmed against primary-source equations.
- No 2024 papers were surfaced as introducing a *new* attack class requiring more rounds (the one 2023/2024-era paper noted, "Algebraic Cryptanalysis of the HADES Design Strategy," eprint 2023/537, appeared only in a search snippet and was not analyzed in depth — flagged as a possible intermediate link between the original design and the 2025-2026 wave, not confirmed).

## Is there a general formula or table relating required R_P to the algebraic degree needed to resist Gröbner-basis/resultant attacks (ideal degree bounds), and how does it compare across these papers?

### Takeaway
The field has converged on expressing Gröbner-basis/resultant attack complexity in terms of the **ideal degree** (also called the "degree of regularity" / "solving degree" proxy) of the polynomial system generated by r rounds of the permutation, with the original Poseidon paper's Eq. (5)-(6) being the first instance of this style of bound, and Poseidon2b, CheapLunch (2025/2040), and the resultant-based bounty papers (2026/150, 2026/1905) explicitly adopting "ideal degree" as the now-standard complexity measure, each contributing tighter upper/lower bounds on it for specific sub-cases (round-reduced, CICO-2, bivariate-resultant-amenable systems) rather than proposing a single unified replacement table.

### Cited Findings
- Original Poseidon Gröbner-basis bound ties R (rounds) directly to a degree-driven inequality: for S(x)=xᵅ, R ≤ log_α(2)·min{M/3, log₂(p)/2}, reflecting that the attack complexity is estimated via "the degree of the reduced-round permutations and their inverses," i.e., an explicit degree-to-round mapping rather than a separate "ideal degree table." — [Poseidon, eprint 2019/458, §5.5.2](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/458)
- Poseidon2b explicitly reframes its whole algebraic-attack reanalysis around this concept: "We base the attack complexity on the ideal degree bound, which is now becoming a standard in Gröbner basis cryptanalysis." — [Poseidon(2)b, §1.1](https://cic.iacr.org/p/2/4/15/pdf)
- CheapLunch (2025/2040) derives "improved complexity estimates for the change of ordering step, derived from tighter upper bounds on the ideal degree," applied across Poseidon, Neptune, and XHash8 — i.e. a cross-primitive tightening of the ideal-degree upper bound, not a new R_P formula per se but a correction to how the *existing* R_P (as fixed by designers) translates into attack complexity. — [eprint.iacr.org/2025/2040](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/2040)
- Bivariate resultant attack (2026/1905) reframes the cost not purely in terms of ideal degree but in terms of the resultant degree D_I and matrix-multiplication exponent w, giving complexity quasi-linear in D_I^(1−1/w) — a different (resultant-degree-based) complexity currency than the Gröbner ideal-degree framing, suggesting the field has not fully standardized on one single degree notion even as of late 2026, though both converge conceptually on "effective polynomial-system degree" as the controlling parameter. — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/1905](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1905)
- Slipway's central empirical result is itself phrased exactly as an ideal/solving-degree comparison: observed degree 3¹⁰ vs. generically expected 3²⁸ for a specific KoalaBear CICO-2 family — i.e. the paper's contribution is precisely a tighter (lower) ideal-degree bound for a structured sub-case that the original per-round "degree roughly multiplies by α each round" heuristic underestimates as an attack surface. — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/1579](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1579)
- Rescue-Prime's own (non-Poseidon, comparandum) round/degree relation: Gröbner-basis-attack-resistant rounds satisfy r ≥ ⌈(s−21)/8⌉ plus a security margin that was reduced from 100% to 50% over the design's revisions, and max polynomial degree for r-round Rescue-Prime systems is 3^(3r−3) — included for cross-family comparison since Rescue-Prime uses a different (non-partial-round) SPN structure but the same general "degree bound ⟹ minimum rounds" methodology. — [Rescue-Prime: a Standard Specification (SoK), eprint 2020/1143, via search summary](https://eprint.iacr.org/2020/1143.pdf)

### Inferences
- There is no single cross-paper "master table" mapping R_P → required ideal degree uniformly across Poseidon, Poseidon2, Poseidon2b, Rescue-Prime, Griffin, Neptune, and GMiMC in the sources retrieved; instead each family/paper computes its own degree-growth recursion specific to its round function (power S-box composed with its specific partial/full layer structure) and each 2025-2026 attack paper's contribution is a *correction* to that family's own ideal-degree-vs-rounds curve for specific constrained sub-problems, not a universal formula.
- The overall trend across 2025-2026 papers is methodological convergence (ideal degree as the shared currency for comparing attacks) even though the concrete bounds/attacks remain primitive-specific and sub-problem-specific (CICO-1, CICO-2, preimage, collision all have different effective ideal-degree requirements, as made explicit by Skipping Class's finding that preimage can be easier than CICO and collision easier than preimage-attack-complexity for Poseidon2(b)).
