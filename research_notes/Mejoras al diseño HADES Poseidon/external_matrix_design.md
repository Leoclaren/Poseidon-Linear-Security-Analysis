# External/Full-Round Linear Layer (M_E) Design — Best Practices Beyond Poseidon2's Kronecker Structure (2024-2026)

Scope note: PDF full-text of several eprint papers (2026/306, 2026/1901, 2026/1931) returned HTTP 403 / CAPTCHA to automated fetchers, so findings for those papers rely on: (a) their HTML abstract pages (which did render), (b) search-engine snippets that quote the PDF text, and (c) a secondary source (zkm.io blog) that appears to have read the full PDF and quotes it directly. Where a claim is sourced only from a snippet/secondary source rather than the primary PDF, this is flagged explicitly.

## Has any paper after Merz & Rodriguez Garcia (eprint 2026/306) formally proven the swapped-order M_E = M_4 ⊗ P_(t/4) secure against ALL round-skipping attacks of this class, or just absence of the exhibited attack?

### Takeaway
No formal/general security proof for the swapped-order (or any other) fix was found anywhere in the 2024-2026 literature surveyed. The paper itself (2026/306) and the follow-up discussion found only claim the attacks "can be mitigated without affecting efficiency" and present several ad hoc repair paths (reordering the Kronecker factors, reverting to MDS, circulant Reed–Solomon MDS layers, adding rounds, or hybridizing Poseidon1/Poseidon2). No paper was found that proves any of these repairs immune to *all* attacks in this class — every source characterizes the status as "no known attack," not a proof.

### Cited Findings
- eprint 2026/306, "Skipping Class: Algebraic Attacks exploiting weak matrices and operation modes of Poseidon2(b)," by Simon-Philipp Merz (ETH Zurich) and Àlex Rodríguez García (UPC), received/approved 2026-02-18 — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/306](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/306)
- The attack "exploited the tensor structure of Poseidon2's external linear layer — specifically, (0, 1, -1, 0) is an eigenvector of its left factor matrix, so a +/-X difference pattern passes through the linear layer round after round without diffusing" — quoted via search snippet of the paper, [eprint.iacr.org/2026/306.pdf](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/306.pdf)
- For t = 16, Poseidon2's external matrix has the form M_E = P ⊗ M, where P (the left/outer Kronecker factor) has the low-weight eigenvector (0, 1, −1, 0) — [search snippet of eprint 2026/306.pdf](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/306.pdf)
- Concrete security-margin impact: for the 31-bit, t = 16, α = 3 instance the Gröbner-basis preimage estimate drops from 2^164 to 2^126; for t = 24 it drops from 2^174 to 2^123 — both below the 2^128 target — [zkm.io blog, "Why Ethereum Walked Away from Poseidon"](https://www.zkm.io/blog/why-ethereum-walked-away-from-poseidon), corroborated by search snippet of [eprint 2026/306.pdf](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/306.pdf)
- The authors also describe "the first algebraic collision attack that outperforms its algebraic preimage counterpart," and for one recommended 128-bit parameter set improve over prior state-of-the-art algebraic collision attacks by a factor of 2^106 — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/306](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/306)
- Per the zkm.io blog, the authors "broke the largest instance in Ethereum's bounty program in under 6 hours on specialized hardware" using a ~2^18.5 speedup over prior methods — [zkm.io blog](https://www.zkm.io/blog/why-ethereum-walked-away-from-poseidon)
- Per the same blog, the paper's own text states the attacks can be mitigated "without affecting the efficiency of Poseidon2(b)" but does not itself assert a security proof for any specific fix — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/306](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/306); [zkm.io blog](https://www.zkm.io/blog/why-ethereum-walked-away-from-poseidon)
- Repair paths discussed in the community/security-review response (per zkm.io blog, attributed to discussion involving Khovratovich and others): (1) "switch back to MDS matrices" — MDS linear layers are argued to prevent four-round skips; (2) "circulant MDS layers built from Reed-Solomon codes with manageable performance loss"; (3) "adding full rounds at roughly 15% overhead"; (4) "a hybrid of Poseidon1 full rounds with Poseidon2 partial rounds" — [zkm.io blog](https://www.zkm.io/blog/why-ethereum-walked-away-from-poseidon)
- A related, independently discovered round-skipping attack by Bariant et al. affects both Poseidon and Poseidon2; because Poseidon2 applies an initial linear layer, its advantage is reduced to only 1 round — [search snippet, likely referencing eprint.iacr.org/2026/1692 or a related paper](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1692)
- A Ethereum Research forum post ("Poseidon2b is secure!", Aug 31 2026, author "ignotusnemo") argues a specific t=4, GF(2^128), 8 full/58 partial round, x^7 S-box Poseidon2b instance resists the 2026/306 attack because "its main wide-state round-skipping method applies to widths t = 12, 16, 20, 24. It does not apply to this t = 4 instance," with the specialized attack complexity estimated at ~2^409.87, well above 128-bit security — [ethresear.ch/t/poseidon2b-is-secure/25865](https://ethresear.ch/t/poseidon2b-is-secure/25865)
- The same forum post explicitly frames this as empirical, not proven, security: "I am not claiming that no better attack can ever be found. I am claiming something checkable: The current published algebraic attacks do not break the exact Poseidon2b instance" — [ethresear.ch/t/poseidon2b-is-secure/25865](https://ethresear.ch/t/poseidon2b-is-secure/25865)
- No citing/follow-up paper proving the swapped-order fix (or any fix) secure against the whole attack class was found. Follow-up eprint papers checked that are topically adjacent — 2026/1692 "From Round Skipping to S-Box Skipping: Attacking Poseidon's Partial Layer via Subspace Restriction" and 2025/954 "Poseidon and Neptune: Gröbner Basis Cryptanalysis Exploiting Subspace Trails" — do **not** cite or discuss eprint 2026/306's matrix-structure attack or its mitigations (confirmed by reading their abstract pages) — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/1692](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1692); [eprint.iacr.org/2025/954](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/954)

### Inferences
- The "swap the factor order" fix described in the task brief (M_E = M_4 ⊗ P_(t/4) instead of P_(t/4) ⊗ M_4) is consistent with the mechanism described in the paper: the vulnerable eigenvector (0,1,-1,0) belongs to the *outer/left* Kronecker factor P, so making the small MDS-like block M_4 the dominant/outer factor (or otherwise ensuring the outer factor has no low-weight eigenvector) removes the specific distinguisher exhibited in the paper. However, since no formal proof was located, this should be treated as a patch against the *known* attack, not a structural guarantee — it plausibly only moves the weak eigenvector problem to whichever factor still has small, structured blocks, unless that factor itself is separately verified eigenvector-free.
- The fact that community mitigation discussion (per the zkm.io summary) defaults to "revert to MDS," "use circulant Reed-Solomon MDS," or "add rounds" — rather than "use the reordered Kronecker matrix" — suggests that even practitioners did not treat the reordering trick as a trusted long-term fix, but rather reached for conservative, previously-analyzed structures (full MDS) or brute-force security margin (more rounds).

### Gaps
- Could not access the full PDF of eprint 2026/306 directly (403/CAPTCHA on direct fetch and via proxy), so the exact wording of the paper's own mitigation section (and whether "swap the factor order" is literally the paper's own proposal vs. a third party's) could not be independently verified beyond the task brief's framing and the zkm.io blog's paraphrase.
- No eprint/arXiv/conference paper was found that formally proves (via a theorem, reduction, or exhaustive structural argument) that any single proposed fix — reordered Kronecker, circulant Reed-Solomon MDS, or added rounds — resists all round-skipping/subspace-trail attacks in this class, as opposed to just the attack instances exhibited in 2026/306. This appears to be a genuine open problem as of November 2026.

## What does Poseidon(2)b (eprint 2025/1893 / IACR CiC 2(4), 2026, Grassi et al.) say about external matrix design, and does it share Poseidon2's tensor-structure vulnerability?

### Takeaway
Poseidon(2)b defines two binary-field variants: **Poseidonb**, which keeps a conservative (MDS) external matrix, and **Poseidon2b**, which — like prime-field Poseidon2 — uses an "aggressive," efficiency-optimized, tensor/Kronecker-structured non-MDS-admitting external matrix. Because Poseidon2b reuses essentially the same Kronecker construction philosophy as Poseidon2, the Merz/Rodriguez-Garcia 2026/306 attack and its underlying weak-eigenvector mechanism apply to Poseidon2b as well (the paper's title explicitly includes "Poseidon2(b)"), whereas Poseidonb's MDS choice is positioned as the safer, if costlier, option.

### Cited Findings
- Title and venue: "Poseidon(2)b: Binary Field Versions of Poseidon/Poseidon2," Lorenzo Grassi et al., eprint 2025/1893, published in IACR Communications in Cryptology, Vol. 2, No. 4 (2026), 45 pages — [eprint.iacr.org/2025/1893](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1893); [cic.iacr.org/p/2/4/15](https://cic.iacr.org/p/2/4/15)
- Abstract: "We present Poseidonb and Poseidon2b, natural variants of Poseidon and Poseidon2, respectively, defined over binary extension fields with a target security level of 128 bits. They are specifically designed to inherit many of the circuit-friendly properties of their prime field version, and to be used together with binary extension field proving systems such as Binius." — [eprint.iacr.org/2025/1893](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1893)
- "Benchmarking demonstrates the merits in proof size, proving time, and especially verification time, in comparison to traditional hash functions and other binary circuit-friendly hash functions such as Vision-32b and Anemoi." — [eprint.iacr.org/2025/1893](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1893)
- "We revisit the security analysis to incorporate recent advances in cryptanalysis and to account for attack vectors that do not arise in the prime field setting. In particular, we focus on algebraic cryptanalysis and subspace trails." — [eprint.iacr.org/2025/1893](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1893)
- Via an initial WebSearch synthesis of the PDF: "Poseidonb and Poseidon2b differ only in their choice of external linear layers M_E for the external rounds. M_E is an invertible t × t matrix, and if n = 128, M_E is defined as an MDS matrix for both Poseidonb and Poseidon2b" — this snippet conflicts with a later search snippet (below) stating Poseidon2b uses non-MDS matrices; see contradiction note below.
- A later, independent search snippet states: "While Poseidonb adopts conservative MDS matrices, Poseidon2b employs more aggressive non-MDS matrices to improve efficiency. The tensor structure of these matrices enables interpretation of the Poseidon2(b) permutation in a particular way" — [search snippet referencing eprint.iacr.org/2025/1893.pdf and eprint.iacr.org/2026/306](https://eprint.iacr.org/2025/1893.pdf)
- The 2026/306 paper's own title — "...weak matrices and operation modes of Poseidon2(**b**)" — explicitly scopes its attack to include the binary-field Poseidon2b variant, confirming the tensor-structure weakness is shared — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/306](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/306)

### Inferences
- The two WebSearch-tool paraphrases of the same underlying PDF directly contradict each other on whether Poseidon2b's M_E is MDS (first snippet: "M_E is defined as an MDS matrix for both Poseidonb and Poseidon2b"; second snippet: "Poseidon2b employs more aggressive non-MDS matrices"). Given that (a) the whole point of Poseidon2 over Poseidon1 is a cheaper, non-MDS-admitting-structure external layer, and (b) the 2026/306 attack paper explicitly targets "Poseidon2(b)" by name for exactly this matrix-structure weakness, the second snippet (non-MDS, tensor-structured, shared vulnerability) is almost certainly the correct reading, and the first snippet is likely a summarization error by the search tool's underlying model. This is flagged as a direct source conflict per instructions rather than silently resolved.

### Gaps
- Could not access the full 2025/1893 PDF directly (403 error) to confirm the exact mathematical definition of Poseidon2b's M_E (i.e., whether it is literally M_4 ⊗ P_(t/4)-style Kronecker, or some other binary-field-specific analog) or to resolve the MDS/non-MDS contradiction above with a verbatim quote.
- Could not determine whether Poseidon(2)b's own security analysis (the "subspace trails" section referenced in the abstract) already anticipated/addressed the eigenvector weakness later exploited by 2026/306, or whether 2026/306 was a genuine post-publication break of Poseidon2b's stated security claims.

## Alternative full-round linear-layer constructions in Griffin, Anemoi, Neptune, or other HADES-family papers that avoid a Kronecker/tensor structure while achieving sub-O(t²) multiplication cost

### Takeaway
Three concrete non-Kronecker alternatives were found: Griffin's **circulant** external matrix (cir(2,1,1) for t=3, generalized for other t), and Neptune's redesigned external round, which replaces the full S-box layer with independent smaller S-boxes over F_p² and uses a matrix construction that costs **t²/2** multiplications instead of Poseidon's t² — a 2x reduction without using a Kronecker product. No fully sub-quadratic (e.g., O(t log t)) non-Kronecker construction free of known issues was found in the sources accessed; Poseidon2's Kronecker/tensor approach remains the main sub-quadratic-cost (O(t) for the diagonal-plus-structure internal matrix, and the block-structured M_E) technique identified, and it is exactly the one shown vulnerable.

### Cited Findings
- Griffin: "The linear layer of Griffin for t = 3 is based on the circulant matrix cir(2, 1, 1)" — [Kudelski Security Research Center, "Implementing ZK-focused hash schemes"](https://kudelskisecurity.com/research/implementing-zk-focused-hash-schemes)
- Griffin: "Each round function of Griffin-π consists of a nonlinear layer, the addition of a round constant, and a linear layer defined by multiplication by an MDS matrix ensuring full diffusion. For certain dimensions, Griffin's external matrix follows a circulant structure." — [search snippet of eprint 2022/403, "Horst Meets Fluid-SPN: Griffin for Zero-Knowledge Applications"](https://eprint.iacr.org/2022/403.pdf)
- Neptune: "Neptune's matrix multiplication of each external round costs t²/2 multiplications with constants versus t² multiplications with constants in Poseidon." — [search snippet, likely from eprint on Neptune / SoK 2026/1931](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1931.pdf)
- Neptune: "is a variant of the hash function Poseidon in which power maps in the external rounds are replaced by the concatenation of independent S-boxes over F_p²... Neptune introduces changes affecting both linear and non-linear layers, using two distinct non-linear layers: one for internal partial rounds and a new one for external full rounds, aiming to minimize non-linear operations." — [search snippet referencing Neptune design papers / eprint 2026/1931](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1931.pdf)
- Anemoi: "relies on a new S-box called Flystel and a new mode of operation called Jive" — this is primarily an S-box/mode innovation rather than a distinct M_E construction; no specific sub-quadratic non-Kronecker linear-layer claim for Anemoi was found in accessed sources — [search snippet](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1931.pdf)
- The Tip5 hash function (Neptune.cash, Szepieniec, eprint 2023/107) was found referenced as a related STARK-recursion-friendly design but its linear-layer construction details were not retrieved in this research pass — [eprint.iacr.org/2023/107](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/107)
- A broader SoK exists: "SoK: Zero-Knowledge Friendly Hash Functions over Prime Fields," Clémence Bouvier et al., eprint 2026/1931, which per its abstract conducts "a systematic survey of ZK-friendly hash functions, covering both algorithmic design paradigms and modes of operation," and "consolidates algebraic cryptanalysis techniques and review[s] the third-party cryptanalysis of each design" — this is very likely the single best primary source for a cross-design comparison of M_E constructions, but its full text could not be retrieved (403/CAPTCHA) in this pass — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/1931](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1931)

### Inferences
- Both identified non-Kronecker alternatives (Griffin's circulant matrix, Neptune's t²/2 construction) are not claimed anywhere in the retrieved sources to be sub-O(t²) in the strict sense (e.g., O(t log t) via FFT-friendly circulant multiplication) — Griffin's circulant matrix is still O(t²) for generic t unless implemented via a convolution algorithm, and Neptune's "t²/2" is only a constant-factor (2x) improvement over Poseidon's O(t²), not an asymptotic improvement. This suggests that, as of the sources found, Poseidon2's Kronecker/tensor approach may be the *only* construction in this family claiming genuinely sub-quadratic-in-practice cost for large t, which is also precisely why it is the one found vulnerable — there appears to be a real cost/structure tradeoff where the cheapest known constructions are also the most structurally exposed.

### Gaps
- Full text of the SoK paper (eprint 2026/1931) could not be retrieved; it likely contains the most direct, authoritative, side-by-side comparison of M_E constructions and their multiplication counts across Poseidon/Poseidon2/Griffin/Anemoi/Neptune, and should be treated as the primary follow-up source if full-text access can be obtained (e.g., manually, or via an authenticated/non-bot-flagged fetch).
- No concrete non-Kronecker construction achieving true asymptotic sub-O(t²) cost (e.g., via FFT/NTT-based circulant multiplication, or low-rank structure) was confirmed in any 2024-2026 HADES-family paper in the sources accessed. This should be flagged as a gap rather than assumed not to exist.
- Anemoi's linear-layer (M_E) construction specifically was not characterized beyond a generic mention; its S-box (Flystel) and Jive mode are well documented in search snippets, but its matrix structure was not confirmed.

## Is there a known general theorem characterizing WHEN a structured matrix (circulant, Kronecker, Toeplitz, etc.) is safe to use as a full-round matrix, as a function of its algebraic structure?

### Takeaway
No domain-specific general theorem (i.e., a cryptography paper proving necessary-and-sufficient algebraic conditions on a structured matrix's safety as a HADES-style M_E) was found. The only identified underlying principle is the basic linear-algebra fact — exploited directly by both the 2026/306 attack and by the user's own CLAUDE.md invariant-subspace proof for Poseidon1 partial rounds — that **any eigenvector of a linear map spans an invariant subspace**, and if that eigenvector has low Hamming weight (i.e., is supported on few coordinates, especially ones that bypass the active S-box position(s)), it yields an exploitable differential/algebraic trail. No paper was found elevating this to a general "safe iff no low-weight eigenvector in any block/factor" theorem specific to circulant, Kronecker, or Toeplitz matrices in the ZK-hash setting.

### Cited Findings
- Generic linear algebra fact (not crypto-specific): "A one-dimensional subspace is T-invariant if and only if T(v) lies in that subspace for all scalars r, which holds if and only if T(v) = λv for some eigenvalue λ... each eigenspace of a linear operator is a T-invariant subspace... any invariant subspace of a diagonalizable matrix is spanned by a subset of the right eigenvectors." — [general linear algebra reference, math.libretexts.org](https://math.libretexts.org/Bookshelves/Linear_Algebra/Linear_Algebra_with_Applications_(Nicholson)/09:_Change_of_Basis/9.03:_Invariant_Subspaces_and_Direct_Sums)
- The 2026/306 attack mechanism is exactly an instance of this: "(0, 1, -1, 0) is an eigenvector of its left factor matrix, so a +/-X difference pattern passes through the linear layer round after round without diffusing" — [eprint 2026/306 via zkm.io blog](https://www.zkm.io/blog/why-ethereum-walked-away-from-poseidon)
- No search for a named theorem ("HADES matrix theorem," "eigenvector safety criterion for AO linear layers," etc.) returned any cryptography-specific result; results returned only generic linear-algebra textbook material — [search attempt](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1306.0083)
- A related, narrower known result (mentioned in the 2024-2026 Poseidon2 mitigation discussion, per the zkm.io blog) is that **full MDS linear layers provably prevent certain round-skip attacks** — attributed to Khovratovich in the blog's paraphrase: "switch back to MDS matrices (Khovratovich showed the four-round skips cannot exist under an MDS linear layer)" — this is a narrower, attack-specific non-existence result, not a general structural safety theorem for arbitrary structured matrices — [zkm.io blog](https://www.zkm.io/blog/why-ethereum-walked-away-from-poseidon)

### Inferences
- The branch-number / MDS criterion (B(M) ≥ t+1) used throughout the CLAUDE.md proof and the broader literature functions as the closest thing to a general sufficient condition: an MDS matrix has no low-weight eigenvectors/invariant coordinate subspaces by construction (every nonzero input difference of weight w produces an output difference of weight ≥ t+1-w, which rules out the kind of localized, S-box-avoiding invariant subspace exploited in both the CLAUDE.md Poseidon1 argument and the 2026/306 Poseidon2 attack). However, this is a sufficient-condition folklore result applied narrowly case-by-case (MDS ⟹ no invariant subspace of this form), not a general theorem that characterizes exactly which structured (circulant/Kronecker/Toeplitz) non-MDS matrices are nonetheless safe. No source provides necessary-and-sufficient conditions for the non-MDS case.

### Gaps
- No general, named theorem was found. This should be reported to the report-writer as an open gap in the literature as of November 2026: safety of structured full-round matrices is currently verified case-by-case (does this specific matrix have a low-weight eigenvector/invariant subspace?) rather than via a general closed-form criterion on the matrix's algebraic structure (e.g., a condition purely in terms of being circulant, Kronecker, or Toeplitz).
- The SoK paper (eprint 2026/1931) is the most likely candidate to contain such a unifying theorem or at least a systematized checklist, if one exists in the literature; full text was not accessible in this research pass.

## Does "Nothing Up My Matrix: Kleptographic Backdoors in ZK-friendly Hash Functions" (eprint 2026/1901) have specific recommendations for generating/verifying a backdoor-free full-round matrix, beyond generic nothing-up-my-sleeve seeding?

### Takeaway
The paper (Hyunsik Jeong, Malte Sander Leip, Mincheol Son) demonstrates that a malicious designer with matrix-selection freedom can plant a matrix that is fully MDS and passes all standard security checks yet contains a kleptographic backdoor mapping a chosen input to a chosen output — with demonstrated real-world consequences for Plonky3, Plonky2, and Neptune Cash. Its core, explicitly stated recommendation is that passing MDS/standard checks is *not sufficient*, and that the matrix's **generation process itself must be transparent and verifiable** — i.e., process-level (not just output-property) verifiability. No Kronecker/tensor-specific discussion was retrievable from the accessible excerpt.

### Cited Findings
- Title/authors: "Nothing Up My Matrix: Kleptographic Backdoors in ZK-friendly Hash Functions," Hyunsik Jeong, Malte Sander Leip, Mincheol Son, eprint 2026/1901 — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/1901](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1901)
- "If a malicious designer controls matrix selection, they can choose a matrix that maps a chosen input to a chosen output under appropriate conditions, with the resulting matrix being MDS and passing the additional matrix security checks required by the target primitive." — [search snippet of eprint 2026/1901.pdf](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1901.pdf)
- "Three case studies show that such a backdoor could have critical security consequences in real-world deployments, including in Plonky3 (where it would allow a prover to control a Fiat-Shamir challenge) and Plonky2 (where it would enable a forged Merkle-tree membership proof)" — also naming Neptune Cash as a third case study per the WebFetch abstract pass — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/1901](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1901)
- Core stated recommendation, via WebFetch of the abstract page: "Its generation process must also be transparent and verifiable." The paper explicitly notes that "satisfying MDS conditions and other security requirements alone is insufficient." — [eprint.iacr.org/2026/1901](https://eprint.iacr.org/2026/1901)

### Inferences
- This paper's finding directly generalizes/motivates the kind of verifiable, non-arbitrary matrix generation that nothing-up-my-sleeve (NUMS) seeding is meant to achieve, but goes further by showing that *output-side* checks (MDS, branch number, standard security criteria) are insufficient as a defense against an adversarial designer — the defense must be about the *process* (reproducible derivation from a public, unmanipulable seed, e.g. hash-of-constant-string-derived parameters as in Poseidon's own Grain LFSR-based constant generation) being independently re-derivable and re-checkable by any third party, not merely about the resulting matrix satisfying algebraic properties.

### Gaps
- Full PDF text of 2026/1901 could not be retrieved (403/CAPTCHA on direct and proxy fetch), so the paper's complete, concrete checklist/algorithm for matrix verification (if one exists beyond the single "transparent and verifiable generation process" statement) could not be extracted. In particular, it is unconfirmed whether the paper makes any statement specific to Kronecker/tensor-structured matrices (like Poseidon2's M_E) as a backdoor vector, or whether its three case studies (Plonky3, Plonky2, Neptune Cash) involve Kronecker-structured matrices at all — this is a meaningful gap given the task's specific interest in tensor-structure risk.
- It is unconfirmed whether this paper cites or relates to eprint 2026/306 (the Merz/Rodriguez-Garcia tensor-eigenvector attack) — i.e., whether it treats structural eigenvector weaknesses (even unintentional, non-backdoored ones) as part of the same "insufficient verification" problem, or treats kleptographic backdoors as an entirely separate concern from accidental structural weakness.

## Reference implementation repos — how are full-round matrices currently generated/verified in practice?

### Takeaway
The HorizenLabs/poseidon2 reference repository generates and documents Poseidon2 matrices via a public Sage script, and its issue/PR history shows the ecosystem treating matrix-generation bugs (not just theoretical weaknesses) as live, patchable problems — consistent with the "Nothing Up My Matrix" paper's point that generation-process transparency (and bug-free reproducibility) matters as much as the matrix's abstract algebraic properties.

### Cited Findings
- The HorizenLabs reference repository (github.com/HorizenLabs/poseidon2) includes a `poseidon2_rust_params.sage` script used to generate Poseidon2 instance parameters, including the external matrix; for t=3 the external matrix is cir(2,1,1) — [github.com/HorizenLabs/poseidon2](https://github.com/HorizenLabs/poseidon2)
- "A bug was fixed which occurred in the computation of the external matrix multiplication when t=4" in this parameter-generation tooling, and a separate fix changed the S-box convention (SBOX=1 → SBOX=0) to match original Poseidon instances — [search snippet referencing github.com/HorizenLabs/poseidon2 history](https://github.com/HorizenLabs/poseidon2)
- Downstream implementations (e.g., Consensys-Incorporated/gnark-crypto PR #912 "Expose the external and internal matrices," PR #929 "replace invalid Poseidon2 diagonals") show ongoing, independent third-party verification/patching of Poseidon2 matrix parameters across the ecosystem in 2025-2026 — [github.com/Consensys-Incorporated/gnark-crypto/pull/912](https://github.com/Consensys-Incorporated/gnark-crypto/pull/912); [github.com/Consensys-Incorporated/gnark-crypto/pull/929](https://github.com/Consensys-Incorporated/gnark-crypto/pull/929)

### Inferences
- The visible pattern (bug fixes to matrix-generation scripts, independent re-derivation/patching by third-party libraries like gnark-crypto) is itself informal evidence supporting the "Nothing Up My Matrix" paper's thesis: real deployments rely on community re-verification of the generation *process* as their practical backdoor/bug defense, rather than on any single formal theorem guaranteeing safety of the resulting matrix's structure.

### Gaps
- Did not locate iden3's specific Poseidon matrix-generation/verification tooling or documentation in this research pass (time/tool-call budget prioritized the primary eprint sources); this should be checked separately if iden3-specific practice is needed.
- Did not confirm whether HorizenLabs' current (November 2026) released parameters have been updated in response to eprint 2026/306's attack (i.e., whether a "swapped order" or MDS-reverted matrix has already shipped in the reference implementation).
